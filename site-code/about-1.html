about

this is guerrilla whooping.

Static Massive is an FPV lifestyle brand born out of it.

transmitting for the people.

It started with analog goggles, a bag of tinywhoops and whatever spot was open for whooping that night. Downtown highrises, stairwells, bandos, a lipo swap on the curb. The brand puts out apparel, original FPV photography, zines, apps and films, including Kamakaze Tsunami, a guerrilla whoop film shot on analog and handycam in Japan.

Tinywhoops and analog are home base, from New York to Los Angeles, Portland, Tokyo and London. 5 inch freestyle, bando and urbex pilots are part of it too. If you've ever threaded a gap you had no business trying, you already get it.

definitionwhat is guerrilla whooping?

An FPV art form, like skateboarding or graffiti, where a tinywhoop dances through the city in places it was never meant to fly.

Guerrilla whooping is flying tinywhoops, tiny ducted FPV drones, through the city the way a skater sees it. The architecture is the course. Grind a handrail. Wall-ride a skyscraper. Thread the hole in the side of a building. Ride the escalators up and down the mall.

It's not racing and there's no field. It's style, flow and lines nobody else saw, filmed in spots that weren't built for it.

You'll see it spelled guerrilla whoop, guerilla whoop or gorilla whoop. Same thing.

field guide · 13 tipshow to guerrilla whoop

01/13
the kit

65mm. 1S. 30,000KV+.

A modern 65 does everything a 75 does, just quieter. Quiet wins busy spots and strict cities like Tokyo.

02/13
the kit

pocket everything.

Goggles around your neck, radio in your top pocket, whoop in the other. You look like a guy out for a walk, not a film crew.

03/13
the kit

travel light.

A small bag with just enough for the day. There's a lot of walking. Sometimes running.

04/13
at the spot

foot level or overhead.

Never head level. Down low or up high, you're just part of the scene. At head height you're in someone's face, and that's how a whoop turns into a problem.

05/13
at the spot

don't be the reason.

Every annoyed person is one more reason to hate drones, and the whole hobby pays for it.

06/13
at the spot

walk it first.

Check every angle and every exit before you plug in.

07/13
at the spot

1–2 packs. in and out.

Hot spot or gnarly line? Crash in the wrong place and that whoop isn't coming back.

don't push your luck.

what to say to the cops.

09/13
when someone shows up

leave before they ask.

Offer to bounce before anyone has to tell you. That's how you get to come back without a trespass.

10/13
when someone shows up

know the zoning.

Malls, plazas and stations have weird rules, and some of it is federal, not city. Move like a ninja. Ask forgiveness, not permission.

11/13
the mindset

whoops get lost.

Don't expect to bring one home, or a working one. If you pushed it, you're getting better.

12/13
the mindset

nobody's looking.

The bigger the city, the less anyone cares. Tokyo is the exception.

13/13
the mindset

make it easy to follow you.

QR code on the back of your stickers, or an NFC tap card. Fastest way to swap handles at the spot.
